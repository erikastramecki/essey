05The 8-week arc

Four acts that build to a reveal

The story isn't random threads — it's a sequence. Establish who we are, prove the foundation, detonate the breakthrough, then point at the future. Week 5 is the peak: the demo reveal.

▸ Flagship: "We publish the dirty audits"
Weeks 5–6 · Act III

The portable proof + the one-command demo video. The peak. This is the tweet that's built to travel. Everything before earns the right to it.

Then loop: the arc is a template, not a one-shot. Re-run it with new proof points (mainnet testnet, audit results, first live loan) as milestones land.

06Weekly rhythm

The default week

Consistency beats volume. This cadence is sustainable for a small team and trains the algorithm and your followers to expect you. Everything is postable in <30 min except the Monday thread.

Mon
Flagship thread — the week's one big idea (from the arc). Post 9–11am ET. This is 60% of the week's reach.
Tue
Reply day — no original post. 30 min replying in ZK / RWA / Sui / DeFi conversations. Be useful, not promotional.
Wed
Explainer — one single tweet + a visual (a screenshot, the node diagram, a gas number). Digestible, saveable.
Thu
Take — quote-tweet a relevant ecosystem post with a sharp POV that positions Essey. Fastest reach lever.
Fri
Build update — what shipped this week, one line, link to the repo. Proof of momentum.
Sat/Sun
One lighter post — a thesis one-liner or a poll — plus ambient engagement. Don't go dark; don't grind.

09Growth tactics

How the account actually grows

  • The demo video is the hero asset. A 30–60s screen recording of `demo.sh` — live price to on-chain verify — is more convincing than any thread. Cut it once, reuse it in every act. Caption it so it works muted.
  • Reply, don't broadcast. From ~0 followers, thoughtful replies in Pyth / Wormhole / Sui / ZK / RWA threads reach more of the right people than your own posts. 30 min/day, be genuinely useful, drop the pitch.
  • Quote-tweet the ecosystem into your narrative. Every Pyth, Wormhole, Robinhood, or new-L2 post is a chance to say "here's why that matters for portable credit." You borrow their reach; they get a smart take.
  • Consistency is the algorithm. The Monday-thread rhythm, held for 8 weeks, beats one viral spike. The account that shows up every week reads as a team that ships.
  • Make threads saveable, not just likeable. A clear diagram, a "what's proven today" table, a numbers tweet — people bookmark those and come back. Bookmarks are the real signal.
  • Engage the sharp replies. When a builder pushes back on the tech, answer in depth in public. Those exchanges are the best content you'll never have to write.
  • Don't buy engagement or chase ratios. The audience you want (funds, builders) can smell it. Substance compounds; farming doesn't.
10Voice & guardrails

How Essey sounds — and the lines we don't cross

Voice

Confident, precise, dry

Technical but translated — a builder should respect it, a trader should get it. Short sentences. Numbers over adjectives. A little dryness is fine; hype is not. Never "gm ser," never "revolutionary," never fake urgency. We sound like people who'd rather show you the repo than the roadmap.

The honesty rules

Non-negotiable guardrails

Never imply it's live, deployed, or on mainnet. It's a research build — say "built" and "demonstrated," not "launched."
Every number is reproducible or it doesn't get posted. If you can't point to `go test` / the repo, don't claim it.
Audits are fix-first. Publish findings after the fix lands; never hide a dirty round; never promise a timeline.

The guardrails aren't a constraint on the brand — they are the brand. "Trust math, not middlemen" only works if we never make the reader trust us on our word.
